Serve receive, rotation by rotation

Where the six stand before the serve, why the overlap rule puts them there, who passes, where the setter releases from, and where everyone goes once the ball is passed. Nothing is typed per rotation: the lineup, the rule and the jobs decide.

The overlap rule, and how it shapes each rotation

FIVB rule 7.4: at the moment the server hits the ball, each front-row player must be at least partly closer to the net than the back-row player in their column, and the three players of each row must keep their left-to-right order. Only those seven pairs matter; there is no rule between a front-row player and a back-row player in another column. After the service hit everyone may move anywhere.

What counts. Positions are judged by the feet touching the floor at the instant of the service hit, with a 0.4 m safety margin here so nobody is standing on the line of legality. A back-row player may be as close to the net as they like as long as the front-row player in their column is closer; a front-row player may stand as deep as they like as long as they stay ahead of their column partner. The setter never passes: they hide behind or beside a teammate as close to the target as the seven pairs allow, then release at the service hit.

The team's rules

Golden rule
R and S never receive. When either is in the back court they are shaded by a teammate: behind a passer, or beside a front-row player at the attack line.
Setter
Sets every second ball from the target. Back row (R6, R1, R2): hides, releases at the service hit, digs right back on defence. Front row (R3, R4, R5): waits at the net, blocks right.
Outsides O1, O2
Pass in every rotation, front row or back. In front they hit from 4 (in R6, O1 hits the first ball from 2 before switching); in the back they cover and dig left back or middle back.
Middles M1, M2
Wait at the net and hit the middle when front row; pass when back row (there is no libero) and dig middle back.
Right side R
Never passes. Front row: waits at the net and hits from 2 (from 4 for the R6 first ball). Back row: tucks behind a passer, covers, and digs right back when the setter is front row.
Numbering
R6 is the starting rotation with the setter serving from zone 1; R1 is one rotation later. The Setter Signals page numbers the same rotations 1–6 starting from the setter in zone 1, so its rotation 1 is R6 here.

Checks

Every rotation is graded against the sheet and the rule: the sheet complete and consistent, every receive formation legal under all seven pairs (recomputed independently of the solver) and matching the sheet's zones and relations, the golden rule kept, the setter at the target before a good pass comes down, every transition on the court, and the same answer every time. Base defence is graded too: the right six spots, a legal stack at our serve, and every switch finished before the other side sets.
