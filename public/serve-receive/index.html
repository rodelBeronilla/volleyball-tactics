The overlap rule, and how it shapes each rotation

FIVB rule 7.4: at the moment the server hits the ball, each front-row player must be at least partly closer to the net than the back-row player in their column, and the three players of each row must keep their left-to-right order. Only those seven pairs matter; there is no rule between a front-row player and a back-row player in another column. After the service hit everyone may move anywhere.

What counts. Positions are judged by the feet touching the floor at the instant of the service hit, with a 0.4 m safety margin here so nobody is standing on the line of legality. A back-row player may be as close to the net as they like as long as the front-row player in their column is closer; a front-row player may stand as deep as they like as long as they stay ahead of their column partner. The libero counts as the back-row middle they replaced. The setter hides as close to the target as the seven pairs allow, then releases at the service hit.

Responsibilities in a 5-1

Setter
Hides as close to the target as the rule allows, releases at the service hit, sets every second ball. Front row (rotations 4–6): blocks right and can dump. Back row: digs zone 1.
Outside hitters
Pass in every rotation (the wing lanes and, with the libero, the centre when the libero is elsewhere), attack the left pin in front, the Pipe, A or B from the back row, and play zone 6 on defence.
Middle
Hides at the net in front, transitions to the centre for the quick and the 2; blocks middle. In the back row the libero plays for them.
Opposite
Does not pass. Hides at the net when front row and attacks the right pin; tucks short at the attack line when back row and attacks the D. Blocks left when front row.
Libero
Passes the centre lane (or a wing when the rotation puts the outsides elsewhere), digs zone 5, never attacks, may not finger-set an attack from inside the attack line.

Checks

Every rotation is graded against the rule and against what a serve-receive formation has to do: legal under all seven pairs (recomputed independently of the solver), the right people passing from a realistic depth, the setter able to reach the target before a good pass comes down, hiders at the net, every transition on the court, and the same answer every time. Base defence is graded too: the right six spots, a legal stack at our serve, and every switch finished before the other side sets.
